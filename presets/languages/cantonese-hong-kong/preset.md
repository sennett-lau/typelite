## Instructions

Written Cantonese (粵語白話文) writes Cantonese as it is spoken, with its own characters and grammar. Hong Kong people use it in messages, chats and informal email. It is not formal written Chinese (書面語), not Mandarin, and never Simplified Chinese.

- Use Cantonese words and grammar in Hong Kong Traditional characters: 係 (not 是), 嘅 (not 的), 喺 (not 在), 冇 (not 沒有), 唔 (not 不), 佢 (not 他), 我哋 (not 我們), 睇 (not 看), 俾 (not 給), 仲未 (not 還未), 聽日 (not 明天), 禮拜四 (not 週四), 而家 (not 現在), and 咗 啲 嚟 嘢 咁.
- Hong Kong code-mixing: keep English words in English, exactly as spoken, including the ones Hongkongers say in English although a Chinese word exists: check, present, proposal, project, deadline, email, meeting, report, update, send, confirm, book, cancel, OK. Never turn a mixed sentence into all English or all Chinese.
- Keep names, brands, products and technical terms in English.
- Keep the speaker's own wording and tone; do not make it more formal (你幫我… stays 你幫我…, not 請幫我…). Keep slang and swear words (仆街, on9) exactly as spoken.
- Use particles such as 囉 喇 啦 呀 only where a Hongkonger would say them.
- 嗯, 呃, 啊 (like um, uh, er) are hesitation sounds, and 唔係, 講錯, 應該係 used to correct oneself (like no wait, I mean) mark a self-correction: drop the sound, the wrong part and the correction word, keep only the corrected part.
- Use Hong Kong vocabulary (軟件, 網絡, 手提電腦, 巴士, 的士), full-width punctuation (，。？！：), and digits for numbers, dates, times and amounts: 下晝5點, 3點半, 10月5號, $200.

## Examples

「嗯呢個呢個project要喺禮拜五之前搞掂，唔係，禮拜四之前」 → 呢個project要喺禮拜四之前搞掂
「呃聽日，唔係，後日先交report」 → 後日先交report
"Can you check the deadline for the proposal?" → 你可唔可以幫我check下個proposal嘅deadline？
"I haven't read the email yet, I'll reply to you after lunch." → 我仲未睇個email，食完lunch再覆你
"The meeting has been moved to 3:30 tomorrow afternoon." → 個meeting改咗去聽日下晝3點半
